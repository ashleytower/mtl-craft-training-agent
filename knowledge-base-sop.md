# Le Fou Fou - Workshop SOP & Training Guide

## Workshop Basics & Pro Tips

Has anyone ever made cocktails before? Whether you're a seasoned shaker or a curious newbie, let's dive into the wild world of mixology with some spicy history, pro tips, and a splash of chaos!

## Essential Tools

**Boston Shaker:** Two pieces, no leaks, maximum drama. If you've got a cobbler shaker (the one with three pieces that always jams), toss it. Or don't. But just know we're judging you silently.

**Jigger:** This tiny measuring cup is your cocktail's BFF. Standard size? 2 oz (big side) and 1 oz (small side), with markings for 0.5 oz, 0.75 oz, and 1.5 oz. No eyeballing—unless you want a drink that tastes like regret.

**Hawthorne Strainer:** That weird metal thing with a spring? It's not a torture device. It's for keeping ice shards out of your drink (and your teeth).

## Shaking Techniques

**Why shake?** It's not just for Instagram. Shaking chills, dilutes, and aerates your drink. Every cocktail needs at least an ounce of water (yes, even your "extra strong" margarita).

**Shake like you mean it:** Weak shakes = sad cocktails. Ice should slam the top and bottom of the shaker—think drum solo, not lullaby.

**Working with Egg Whites:** Always start with a dry shake! Hold that shaker TIGHT - without ice, it doesn't seal well and can pop open. This first shake without ice creates that beautiful silky texture and pillowy top we're looking for.

## Pro Tips

- **Start cheap:** You always start with the least expensive ingredients first and you always add the alcohol last, nobody wants to mess up a cocktail with a bottle of $100 tequila!
- **Fresh everything:** Juice your citrus, use our syrups
- **Mint Muddling:** Crush herbs gently. Bruised mint tastes like a rusty spoon. Press, don't pulverize!
- **Every cocktail has a sugar component,** even if there is no syrup a liqueur is the sugar source

## House-Made Syrups

When you explain them, most fruit for example the passion fruit, any Berry, the cactus pear they are all made with a cold process. When you make a strawberry syrup on the stove it ends up tasting a bit like Jam and you lose the brightness of the fruit!

- **Espresso Syrup:** Real Espresso mixed with demerara sugar, simmered slowly. Enhances espresso martinis.
- **Grapefruit Cordial:** Grapefruit peels soaked in sugar overnight, mixed with fresh grapefruit juice, lime, and salt. Perfect for Palomas.
- **Toasted Almond Orgeat:** Almonds toasted, blended into milk, strained, and mixed with sugar. Essential for Mai Tais.
- **Butterfly Pea Syrup:** Butterfly pea flowers soaked for 24 hours, strained. Creates color-changing cocktails.
- **Ginger Syrup:** Fresh ginger juice mixed with raw sugar. Used in various "mule" cocktails.
- **Hibiscus Syrup:** Dried hibiscus flowers steeped with cinnamon. Pairs well with tequila.
- **Passion Fruit Syrup:** Fresh passion fruit pulp mixed with sugar. Popular in mojitos.
- **Grilled Pineapple Syrup:** Whole pineapples grilled, juiced, and mixed with brown sugar. Used in Smoky Coladas.
- **Spiced Cranberry Syrup:** Fresh cranberries simmered with everything that screams Christmas - cinnamon, nutmeg, cloves, star anise, and orange peel.
- **Strawberry/Raspberry Syrup:** Fresh berries macerated with lemon zest and sugar.
- **Mango Syrup:** Ripe mangoes pureed with lime and sugar. Adds tropical flavor.
- **Spiced Pear Syrup:** Pears poached with star anise, then syrup reduced. Great in Bellinis.
- **Jalapeño Syrup:** We make our syrup with fresh jalapenos simmered, Blended and then we put it through a cheesecloth. That's why our spicy margaritas actually taste like jalapenos and not just heat that burns your lips like most bars!
